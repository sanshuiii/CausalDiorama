AWM_PROXY_CONTROL. Several visual references accompany this text. <Video 1> is the conditioning
proxy video: treat it as a coarse spatiotemporal reference, not an appearance template or
literal target geometry. Every <Picture N> is an individual appearance reference assigned to
exactly one subject in subject_definitions; the environment is a separate subject with its own
picture. A subject's identity, appearance, materials, colours and rendering style follow its
assigned picture; the place itself — ground, architecture, scenery, sky, lighting, time of day,
worldview and art direction — follows the environment picture. The pictures provide NOTHING about
position, size, pose, camera, framing, composition or motion: never copy a picture's backdrop,
framing, viewpoint or studio lighting, and never show a subject in front of its reference
backdrop. Each subject's position and motion come from <Video 1>.

Human proxies in <Video 1> provide only framewise root position, coarse occupied scale, and
whole-body root orientation, which are reliable at roughly 80-100% and should be followed
closely. They carry no information about character appearance and do not represent identity,
face, hairstyle, clothing, materials, colors, visual effects, anatomy, bones, joints, limb
articulation, detailed posture, torso or head pose, hand pose, action phase, or physical
contact. Reconstruct each character's appearance from its assigned picture and the timestamped
text, and reconstruct articulated motion from the timestamped text. Never reproduce a human proxy
as a skeleton, stick figure, mannequin, node-connected body, or exposed rig. Left and right
refer to each person's own body unless explicitly stated as screen direction.

Vehicle proxies in <Video 1> provide only framewise position, trajectory, body heading,
rotation, and accurate overall length, width, height, and occupied scale; these anchors
should be followed closely. They carry no information about vehicle appearance. Never
reproduce a vehicle proxy as a wireframe vehicle, cage frame, tubular skeleton, exposed
chassis, or low-poly proxy car. Vehicle identity, type, design details, materials, colors,
and visual effects are defined by its assigned picture and the text.

Roads, terrain, buildings, relative depth, rough scale, and layout in <Video 1> provide
denser but still approximate structural cues. The text may reinterpret these cues to create
strongly out-of-distribution scenes while maintaining broad spatial and temporal coherence.
Conditioning colors, proxy geometry, node markers, rigging structures, and other
visualization artifacts must never appear in the final video.

When sources conflict, resolve in this order: (1) geometry, camera, position and motion
follow <Video 1>; (2) identity, appearance and materials of each subject follow its assigned
picture, and the look of the place follows the environment picture; (3) the timestamped text
defines the concrete events, actions and rewrite targets within those constraints.

Clip contract: this clip is the very beginning of the take — nothing precedes it. Its first
frame is not given: compose it from the first frame of <Video 1> — camera, layout, positions
and scale — with every visible subject rendered as in its assigned picture and placed inside
the environment of the environment picture, under one consistent lighting. Photographic, one
continuous shot.
